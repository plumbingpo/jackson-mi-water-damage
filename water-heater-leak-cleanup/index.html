Water Heater Leak Cleanup in Jackson: What to Know

The tank on a water heater stores 40 to 80 gallons, yet a leak can release far more than that. Because the supply line feeds the tank, a rusted out bottom drains and refills at the same pace. Nothing stops the flow except shutting the cold water valve. The location of the heater sets the damage. It may be a hallway closet, a garage floor or, the worst case, an attic over finished rooms.

Our Jackson team extracts the water, then looks at what surrounded the heater. Closet walls and the platform under the tank are usually soaked at the bottom. Garage leaks wet whatever is stored on the floor, along with the wall the garage shares with the house. From an attic it comes down through insulation and ceilings. Our crew takes readings in every area and sets drying equipment to bring it back to normal.

Do This First

What to Do Right Now

  1. Cut the Power or GasElectric units get shut down at the breaker. With a gas heater, the knob on the control goes to the off position. Never leave a heating element or burner running on an empty tank.
  2. Shut the Cold Water ValveFind the cold water shutoff above the heater and turn it clockwise. A stuck or leaking valve means going to the house main and shutting the water off there.
  3. Beware of Scalding WaterTank water is hot enough to scald. Pets and children should stay clear, and nobody should walk into pooled water before it cools down.
  4. Drain the Tank SafelyA garden hose on the drain valve, if you can get to it safely, lets you send what is left in the tank outside or into a floor drain instead of across the floor.
  5. Move Stored ItemsGet rugs, boxes and other absorbent things up off the wet floor. Cardboard and fabric soak up water quickly and hold it against walls and concrete.
Coverage

What About Insurance?

When a tank fails suddenly, the water damage to floors, walls and belongings is often covered as sudden and accidental. The water heater itself usually is not, since wearing out with age is considered maintenance. Because coverage usually does not extend to gradual leaks, a claim may be denied or reduced when the adjuster sees indications the tank leaked for a long time, like old stains or trails of rust. Read your policy, take photos of the damage and the heater, and do not dispose of the old unit before the claim is settled.

What It Costs

What Affects the Cost

  • Condition of the stand or subfloor below the heater, which may need rebuilding before the replacement is set.
  • How long the tank kept refilling before the supply valve was closed.
  • The location of the heater, since a garage leak costs less than one in an interior closet or attic.
  • Amount of contents affected, since wet belongings have to be relocated, dried or discarded.

The Usual Culprits

What Causes It

  • Pressure rising too high in the tank because the thermostat failed or no expansion tank was installed.
  • Years of sediment buildup and corrosion wearing a hole in the base of an old steel tank.
  • An overflow pan that cannot drain, because its line is clogged or the pan itself has rusted.
  • Corroded inlet and outlet fittings or flex connectors on top of the heater.
  • Loose plumbing connections left after a recent installation or repair.
What Is at Risk

What the Water Damages

Shared Garage Wall

As water runs across the garage slab, it wets the drywall and bottom plate of the wall the garage shares with the house and continues into flooring on the other side.

Items Stored Near the Heater

The first casualties are usually the furniture, boxes and keepsakes stored around the heater in a garage or closet. Mold can develop on damp contents that are not promptly dried or tossed, while cardboard boxes disintegrate.

Stand and Subfloor Damage

More water goes into the subfloor or wooden stand beneath the tank than anywhere else. It can rot and weaken, which matters when a new full tank is set on it.

Flooring in Nearby Rooms

From a heater closet, the leak slips beneath the door and reaches hallway hardwood, laminate or carpet. Heat makes it worse, with quicker swelling of wood boards and carpet backing that comes apart.
