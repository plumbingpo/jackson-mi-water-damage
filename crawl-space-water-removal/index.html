Crawl Space Water Removal: What Jackson Homeowners Should Know

Few homeowners notice right away when water collects beneath the floor. Most people find it when the floors feel cold and damp, a musty smell comes up through the vents, or someone opens the access hatch and sees a pond. Usually that means days of moisture in the joists already, plus insulation that is wet all the way through. We pump the space dry, remove the ruined material and get the wood dried while it is still sound.

Pumping is just half of the work. Floor joists, beams and the subfloor absorb moisture slowly and let go of it slowly, so we take moisture readings on the wood and keep dehumidifiers running until the numbers come down, usually 3 to 5 days. Our team traces the source too, whether that is a supply line leak, a drain, or rain driven groundwater, so the space stays dry afterward.

What It Looks Like

  • Insulation that has pulled loose and hangs in wet clumps below the joists
  • Joists, beams or subfloor panels coated with whitish or blackish fuzz
  • A musty, earthy smell that is strongest near the floor vents
  • Stains on the support piers and stem walls that show how high water stood
  • More crickets, roaches or other bugs showing up near the floor level
  • A first floor with a cool, moist feel and some give when you walk on it
If Nothing Is Done

How the Damage Progresses

Day one

Water Pools Below

Water collects in the low spots and soaks into the soil. Humidity in the space jumps within hours as the insulation pulls water into itself.

Hours 24 to 48

Mold Gets Started

Paper faced insulation and moist joists are where mold is able to appear first. The weight of the water drags the batts loose from the underside of the subfloor.

Days 3 through 7

Dampness Rises Indoors

Humid air from below works its way up into the rooms. The lumber goes on taking in moisture, a stale smell spreads and flooring can begin to cup.

After a week

Wood Starts Rotting

Lumber left damp this long starts breaking down. Sill plates and joists lose firmness, nails and hangers corrode, and termites along with other pests move toward the moisture.

What We Do

Our Crawl Space Water Removal Process

1

Safety Check and Inspection

Nobody enters until the space has been screened for shock risks, hanging ducts and contamination. Next comes charting the extent of the water and metering the joists and subfloor for moisture.

2

Pump Out Water

We run pumps and extraction lines until the pooled water is gone, low pockets and trenches along the footings included. While pumping, the crew also cleans off the sediment the water left on the plastic.

3

Clear Out Ruined Material

Soaked insulation, ruined vapor barrier and debris are bagged and carried out. Where mold has begun or is likely, the bare joists and subfloor get cleaned and an antimicrobial applied.

4

Dry and Verify

Low profile air movers and a dehumidifier run under the house until the wood reaches a normal moisture reading. A final meter check follows, and you receive the numbers along with steps to prevent a repeat.

Behind the Surface

The Damage You Do Not See

What You Breathe Upstairs

Much of what you breathe on the main level drifts up from beneath the house, carrying any mold spores and musty smells along with it.

Termites and Other Pests

Rodents, carpenter ants and termites all seek out moist lumber and soggy ground. Drying the space removes much of what attracts them.

Settling Under the House

When the ground stays soaked, footings may lose support and piers can tilt or sink. Sloping floors or sticking doors in the rooms above may point to this.

Weakened Floor Framing

Decay tends to begin along the upper edge of the framing or where it meets the sill, out of view from the access opening. Our crew tests the lumber by hand to locate weak areas.
